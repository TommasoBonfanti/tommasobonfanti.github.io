Optical trapping behind a transparent membrane

Research project, Photonics Lab, ETH Zurich · Feb. 2026 – Jun. 2026

Authors
Tommaso Bonfanti, Jonas Ochsenbein
Supervision
Dr. Louisiane Devaud, Prof. Dr. Lukas Novotny

Abstract

Optically levitated nanoparticles in high vacuum are a versatile platform for testing quantum mechanics at large mass scales. One promising route towards macroscopic quantum superpositions is to let the particle’s wavefunction evolve in a non-harmonic potential created by static fields from nearby electrodes, which must sit close to the trap without blocking the trapping beam. Graphene, a single atomic layer that is electrically conductive but nearly transparent, is an attractive candidate for this role.

This thesis takes a first proof-of-concept step in that direction. We design and build a trapping assembly that positions a glass substrate carrying a graphene membrane on the incoming side of the focus, and demonstrate stable trapping of silica nanoparticles behind both bare and graphene-coated glass. We characterize the resulting focus distortion through the power-normalized centre-of-mass frequencies of the trapped particle and compare it with vectorial simulations of the focal field in a stratified medium. Inserting a substrate weakens the trap most strongly along the optical axis, and the graphene coating adds a further reduction beyond the bare glass. Applying an oscillating voltage to the contacted sample drives the trapped particle, but a control measurement shows that the graphene contribution cannot yet be isolated. In addition, we check whether nanoparticles accumulate on the graphene during trap loading, and find no detectable deposition in microscopy of sprayed samples.

Together, these results are a first step towards using graphene as a transparent electrode in levitated optomechanics.
